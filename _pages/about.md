<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 1.8rem;">Hi there 👋, Welcome to my portfolio website!</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 1.8rem;">I am an early-career public policy and comparative institutional analysis researcher based in Tübingen, Germany, originally from Kathmandu, Nepal. My academic journey spans training in comparative institutional analysis and quantitative survey methods with field and research experience across Nepal, China, and Germany.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 1.8rem;">At the Universität Tübingen, I currently work as a Graduate Student Assistant within Global Awareness Education. In this role, I act as a teaching assistant for global policy courses and coordinate logistical frameworks and content development for international training sessions and university events serving master's and PhD researchers.
I completed my Bachelor of Liberal Arts and Sciences (2015–2021) at Purbanchal University in Kathmandu, Nepal, where I was a first-generation college student. During this time, I also focused on cross-cultural policy through a Fulbright Global Undergraduate Exchange scholarship at Grand Valley State University, Michigan, in 2020. Driven by a responsibility to give back to my community, I spent four years leading civil initiatives and social impact programs as a Program Lead for ‘We’ for Change, a youth-led NGO in Nepal, where I secured over €15,000 from international donors. In 2021, I was honored with the Princess Diana Award for my work in these civic and leadership spaces. In 2022, I was selected as a Schwarzman Scholar at Tsinghua University in Beijing, where I completed my Master of Management Science in Global Affairs with a focus on comparative political contexts. Following this, I moved to Germany in 2023 to pursue my Master of Public Policy and Social Change at the Universität Tübingen.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 1.8rem;">In my Master’s thesis, supervised by Prof. Dr. Martin Seeleib-Kaiser and Prof. Dr. Thomas Diez, I examined why decentralized governance execution remains structurally constrained despite formal legislative local authority. While my empirical focus was a localized case study, what drew me deeper into this field was the classic institutional paradox of formal mandate vs. structural inertia. My findings traced how modern responsibilities are layered onto unreformed financial, technical, and bureaucratic structures, producing a self-reinforcing institutional lock-in that resists state reform even when resources are available. The underlying logic of this thesis is directly transferable to broader public policy debates, such as tracing welfare state dualization across insider/outsider protection regimes in the EU and the West.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 1.8rem;">Over the past few years, my work has increasingly focused on how legacy systems, path dependence, and institutional stickiness reproduce social inequalities and policy implementation gaps. I use a mixed-methods toolkit combining qualitative elite interviews and content analysis (MAXQDA, NVivo) with quantitative statistical methods like multilevel regressions and difference-in-difference modeling (R, Stata). I want to pursue a PhD in public policy or political science broadly focused on these themes.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 1.8rem;">I do not yet have peer-reviewed journal publications. So far, my academic outputs include working policy papers, evaluation reports, and my completed master's thesis framework.</p>
